Everything Hayat knows about access hangs off one tree. A service never stores who may do what: it files its resources in the tree and
asks.

```text title="An instance's tree"
hayat/root                     the instance
├─ hayat/service  billing      a service's namespace; its owners approve its contracts
└─ hayat/tenant   Acme         an organisation every service shares
   ├─ hayat/group Engineering  a folder and a roster at once
   │  ├─ hayat/group Payment
   │  │  └─ billing/ledger  Ledger 2026     a resource billing mirrored here
   │  └─ hayat/group Promo
   └─ hayat/group Marketing
```

## Tenants

A **tenant** is an organisation: a company, a customer, a team that works apart from the others. Every service shares the same
tenants, so Acme in one app is Acme in all of them.

A tenant has one **owner**, who is always a member and can't be removed, and a **roster** of members. Being on the roster is what lets
someone see a tenant's resources at all: for any type that requires membership, a person who isn't on the roster is refused whatever
was granted to them. A tenant can only be deleted when nothing is filed under it.

The owner runs their tenant from the same console as the instance admin (its members, its name, its part of the tree) without being
an instance admin.

## Groups

A **group** is two things at once: a folder resources are filed under, and a list of people a grant can name. Groups hold groups, so
they nest like an org chart.

**Belonging goes up, access goes down.** Whoever is on Payment's roster counts as being in Engineering too, because Payment sits under
it, so a grant *to* Engineering reaches them. But a grant *on* Payment reaches nothing above it, and a team never opens what a sibling
team filed.

Rosters themselves stay flat: a group lists only the people put on it directly. The console shows the rest as "via Payment". A team
lead isn't a column on the roster; it's a role granted on the team's node.

## Agents

An **agent** is a member that isn't a person: an automation or an assistant that holds roles and works in services but never signs in.
Each lives in one tenant and answers to a **sponsor** on its roster; if the sponsor leaves, it pauses. It holds less than a person could:
never ownership, never a grant made to the whole tenant, and never a permission only people may hold. See [Agents](/docs/agents).

## Resources

A **resource** is one of your service's things (a ledger, a database connection, a report) mirrored into the tree as a node. Your
service creates the node when it creates the thing, under a tenant or a group, with an optional owner. Its type comes from your
service's [contract](/docs/contracts), written short in the manifest and qualified in the tree: `ledger` becomes `billing/ledger`.

Hayat only knows a resource's type, id, name, parent and owner. Its contents stay in your service.

## Roles and permissions

A **permission** is a key a service enforces, like `billing.ledger.view`. It exists because a route checks it, and it has a **scope**:
the node type it means something on.

A **role** is a named set of permissions. Services suggest roles in their contracts, Hayat has three of its own, and an admin can make
more in the console, starting from a copy of an existing one.

| Built-in role | Granted on | Lets someone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tenant admin (`hayat.tenant-admin`) | the root or a tenant | Run a tenant: its settings, members, groups, agents, and who may do what in it |
| Group manager (`hayat.group-manager`) | a group | Run one group: its members, what is filed in it, and grants inside it |
| Service owner (`hayat.service-owner`) | a service | Approve contracts that touch its namespace, name its other owners, rotate its API secret |

## Grants

A **grant** gives one role to a person or a group on one node. It counts on that node and, unless it says otherwise, everything below
it. A grant can expire.

A grant is only valid where it can mean something: the role must apply to the node's type, every permission's scope must sit at or
below the node, a person must be on the tenant's roster, and a group must belong to the same tenant (or be the tenant itself, meaning
everyone in it). When someone leaves a tenant, their grants and group memberships in it go with them.

## Decisions

When a service asks whether a person holds a permission on a node, Hayat walks the chain from the root down to that node:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Owner">Owning any node on the chain allows every permission there and below.</Step>
  <Step title="Grants">A grant on the chain counts when it names the person, or a group (or the tenant) they count in, and reaches the node.</Step>
  <Step title="Placement">
    If the resource's type opens by placement, whoever counts in the nearest group above it may use its access permission, and so may
    anyone directly on the roster of a group further up. The tenant never counts here.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Membership">If the type requires membership, someone who isn't on the tenant's roster gets nothing, whatever the steps above said.</Step>
</Steps>

There are no deny rules: access is the union of everything that allows it. That keeps answers cacheable and explainable, and each answer
can name the first thing on the chain that gave it (`owner`, `grant`, `placement` or `admin`). Instance admins hold Hayat's own
`hayat.*` permissions everywhere, but never a service's: administering the instance is not reading its data.

The full rule, with examples, is in [How decisions are made](/docs/access-model).

## Services and contracts

A **service** is an application that files resources in the tree and asks for decisions. An instance admin registers it, which gives
it a namespace (`billing`), API credentials and a first owner. Its **contract** declares its resource types, permissions and suggested
roles. Contracts only ever add, and a new version goes live as soon as it is published, unless it files its types under another
service's types: then that service's owners approve it first. See [Plug a service in](/docs/services).
