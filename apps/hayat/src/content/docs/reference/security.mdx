## Keys

- **Signing keys** are ES256 (P-256). They sign every access and ID token, are published at the issuer's keys endpoint, and rotate every
  `HAYAT_KEY_ROTATION` (90 days by default). The previous key stays published for 48 hours after a rotation so tokens it signed still
  verify.
- **At rest**, the signing keys are encrypted with AES-256-GCM under `HAYAT_MASTER_KEY`. The database alone can't sign a token.

## Passwords

- Hashed with **argon2id**. A hash imported in an older form is replaced with argon2id at that person's next sign-in.
- At least **10 characters**.
- A new account is **pending** until its mailbox answers: the password is chosen from the emailed link, so nobody can sign in with an
  address they don't read.
- Sign-up and password reset never reveal whether an address already has an account.

## Sessions and tokens

- One **session** per sign-in at Hayat, kept in a cookie on the issuer's origin, `Secure` when the issuer is https. It gives single
  sign-on across every app.
- **Disabling** an account or **resetting** its password ends every one of its sessions.
- **Access tokens** carry who someone is (`sub`, `sid`) and never what they may do. Services verify them offline and ask for every
  decision, so a revoked grant takes effect at once.
- **Refresh tokens** rotate on every use. Presenting one that was already used revokes its whole family, and a refresh token dies with
  its session.
- **Public clients** must use PKCE with S256. **Redirect URIs** must be https, except on `localhost`, unless `HAYAT_DEV=true`.

## Rate limits

| Action | Per network address | Per email address |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sign in | 60 a minute | 10 every 15 minutes |
| Sign up | 10 an hour | |
| Reset a password | 10 an hour | 3 an hour |

Counted in Redis when `HAYAT_REDIS_URL` is set, otherwise in each process's memory.

## Admins

- The **last instance admin** can't be demoted or disabled, so an instance is never left without one.
- Instance admins hold Hayat's own permissions, never a service's: administering the instance is not reading its data.
- Every admin change writes an **audit** row (who changed what) in the same transaction as the change.

## Services

- A service authenticates with its own client credentials and may write only nodes of its own namespace.
- A contract version that files types under another service's types waits for that service's owners to approve it.
- A service token is refused where a person's token is expected.
