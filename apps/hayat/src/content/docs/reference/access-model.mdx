Every answer Hayat gives comes from one rule, in one place in its code. Given a person, a permission and a node, it walks the chain from
the root down to that node and stops at the first thing that allows it.

## The rule

<Steps>
  <Step title="Owners">Owning any node on the chain allows every permission there and below.</Step>
  <Step title="Grants">
    A grant counts on its own node always, and below it only when it inherits. It counts when it names the person, or a group (or the
    tenant) they count in. Expired grants don't count.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Placement">
    When the node's type opens by placement, its access permission is held by whoever counts in the nearest group above it (its own
    roster, or a group nested under it), and by whoever is directly on the roster of a group further up. The tenant never counts here.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Membership">A type that requires membership allows nothing to someone who isn't on the tenant's roster, whatever was granted.</Step>
  <Step title="Instance admins">Hold Hayat's own `hayat.*` permissions everywhere, and never a service's.</Step>
  <Step title="Unknown keys">A permission the catalog doesn't know is dropped, never honoured.</Step>
</Steps>

## Belonging goes up, access goes down

Take Acme's org chart, with a ledger filed in Payment:

```text title="Acme"
Acme (tenant)                   owner: alice
└─ Engineering                  roster: bob
   ├─ Payment                   roster: paula, pete, priya
   │  └─ Ledger 2026 (billing/ledger, opens by placement for ledger.view)
   └─ Promo                     roster: leo, mia
```

| Who | May view Ledger 2026? | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| alice | Yes | **Owner** of Acme, a node on the chain. |
| pete | Yes | **Placement**: on Payment, the nearest group above the ledger. |
| bob | Yes | **Placement**: directly on Engineering, a group further up. |
| mia | No | Promo is a sibling of Payment. She counts in Engineering through Promo, but placement only counts direct rosters above the nearest group, so sibling teams never open each other's things. |

Now grant the Accountant role **to** Engineering, **on** the tenant, inheriting. Mia counts in Engineering (through Promo), so the grant
reaches her and she may view the ledger. A grant **on** Payment, by contrast, would reach nothing above Payment.

## Union only

There are no deny rules. Access is the union of everything that allows it, so adding a grant can only open things and removing one can
only close them. That keeps answers cacheable (a cache entry can't be made wrong by an unrelated rule) and explainable.

## Every answer can say why

Ask `Check` with `explain: true` and the answer carries a **reason**: the first thing up the chain that gave the permission, its kind
(`owner`, `grant`, `placement` or `admin`), the node, the role and who it was granted to, and the same in a sentence. The console's
access lists are built from these.

## Caches and revisions

Every policy write bumps a revision in the same transaction: the tenant's, or a global one for the catalog, roles, the root, service
grants and account status. Answers are cached under the revisions they were computed at, so any change in a tenant makes new keys and
old entries are never read again.

Services cache answers too, through the [SDKs](/docs/sdks), and empty them from the Watch stream. To read your own write, a request can
ask for at least a revision (`at_least_rev`); for a destructive action, `fully_consistent` bypasses every cache.

## Lookups agree with checks

`LookupResources` and `LookupSubjects` confirm every candidate with the same rule `Check` uses, so a list never shows something a check
would refuse.
